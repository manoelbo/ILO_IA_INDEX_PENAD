6    Conclusion

This paper provides early, large-scale evidence that the diffusion of GenAI since 2023 is
associated with seniority-biased employment effects within firms. Using résumé and job-
posting data linked to roughly 281,000 U.S. firms, together with a firm-level adoption
measure based on “GenAI integrator” vacancies, we document a decline in junior em-
ployment relative to senior employment at adopting firms compared with non-adopting
firms. This decline is concentrated in high-exposure occupations: within adopting firms,
junior employment in high-exposure occupations contracts relative to low-exposure oc-
cupations after 2022, while the corresponding senior series continues to rise. Task-level
evidence from job postings points to the same mechanism: GenAI-exposed tasks become
less likely to appear in junior task bundles at adopting firms. Finally, decomposing em-
ployment flows shows that the decline is driven primarily by slower hiring rather than
increased separations.
    These findings should be interpreted with caution. Adopting firms differ systemat-
ically in size, workforce composition, and industry. Although our research designs ab-
sorb many observable and unobservable differences across firms, sectors, and time, un-
observed confounding factors may remain. We therefore examine four prominent alterna-
tive explanations: monetary tightening, post-pandemic hiring dynamics, the expansion
of remote work, and labor-supply shocks. These tests support our interpretation, but they
cannot rule out all alternative explanations. In addition, our adoption measure captures
