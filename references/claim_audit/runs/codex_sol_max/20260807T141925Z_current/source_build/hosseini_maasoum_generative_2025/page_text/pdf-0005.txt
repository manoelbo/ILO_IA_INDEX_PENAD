the framework’s assumption that junior tasks are more susceptible to displacement.
   We then turn to the firm-level employment response. We first compare junior and se-
nior employment at adopting and non-adopting firms. From 2018 through 2022, junior
employment evolves similarly in the two groups. Beginning in late 2022, however, ju-
nior employment at adopters diverges sharply downward relative to non-adopters, while
senior employment shows no comparable break. To directly assess the seniority-biased
effect, we estimate a triple-difference specification that compares junior versus senior em-
ployment within adopting firms relative to non-adopters. This design includes firm-by-
time fixed effects, which absorb shocks or trajectories specific to a given firm in a given
period, and industry-by-time-by-seniority fixed effects, which account for sector-level
dynamics that differentially affect juniors and seniors. The coefficients are flat through
2022Q4 and then decline sharply from 2023Q1 onward, reaching roughly a 9 percent de-
cline in junior employment relative to senior employment after six quarters.
