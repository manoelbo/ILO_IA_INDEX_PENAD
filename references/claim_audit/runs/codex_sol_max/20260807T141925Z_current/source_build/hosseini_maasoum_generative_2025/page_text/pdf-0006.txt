    We complement these analyses with a staggered event study that traces employment
dynamics around firms’ GenAI adoption date, proxied by the first period in which the
firm posts a GenAI integrator role. This design helps distinguish adoption effects from
broader time-specific shocks by exploiting variation in the timing of adoption across
firms. However, it is sensitive to measurement error in the adoption timing proxy—for
instance, if firms begin using GenAI before, or only several periods after, posting for an
integrator role. The estimates reveal no evidence of differential pre-trends: employment
evolves similarly in adopting and non-adopting firms throughout the eight quarters pre-
ceding adoption. However, roughly two quarters after adoption, junior employment in
adopting firms begins to decline, reaching an 8 percent reduction after eight quarters.
The absence of significant pre-trends provides additional reassurance that these post-
adoption declines are unlikely to be driven by confounding shocks.
    We next address potential endogeneity concerns. Adopting and non-adopting firms
differ systematically, which could bias our estimates; for example, if adopting firms are
more responsive to other shocks affecting employment. One concern is that GenAI adopters
may have been more sensitive to the 2022–2023 monetary tightening cycle. Several pieces
of evidence suggest that this mechanism does not drive our findings: including industry-
by-seniority-by-time fixed effects leaves the results essentially unchanged; the event-
study estimates show no pre-adoption trends; and a formal test based on the high-frequency
identified monetary-policy surprises of Jarocinski and Karadi (2020), following Ottonello
and Winberry (2020), finds no evidence that adopter firms are more responsive to mon-
etary shocks. This result is also consistent with the literature documenting that larger
firms—which are more likely to adopt GenAI—tend to be less sensitive to monetary pol-
icy (Chodorow-Reich, 2014; Gertler and Gilchrist, 1994).
