    Importantly, to emphasize that these estimates capture differential responses
between exposed and less exposed firms, not absolute changes in hiring patterns.
Hence, a coefficient of zero does not imply that hiring for a particular role remained
unchanged. Rather, it indicates that exposed and less exposed firms adjusted their
demand for that role similarly. For instance, if all firms reduced hiring of software
engineers, regardless of their own exposure levels, our estimates would show no dif-
ferential effect. The significant negative coefficient for e.g. data analysts therefore
reveals that exposed firms reduced analyst hiring beyond any economy-wide reduc-
tion, suggesting that firm-level exposure amplifies the substitution effects already
occurring across the labor market. We next turn to our occupation-level analysis to
examine how hiring intentions of exposed jobs change in the aggregate.
