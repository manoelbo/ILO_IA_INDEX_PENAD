tasks previously done by labor more cheaply, it creates a displacement effect that
reduces labor demand. Conversely, the creation of new tasks where labor main-
tains comparative advantage generates a reinstatement effect that increases labor
demand (Acemoglu and Restrepo, 2019; Restrepo, 2024). For LLMs, which can auto-
mate cognitive tasks previously considered non-routine, the framework predicts that
displacement will dominate in the short run, particularly for entry-level positions
involving routine information processing and basic analysis. Senior roles requiring
tacit knowledge, judgment, and complex interpersonal coordination should prove
less vulnerable. This is consistent with our findings.
    Our findings contribute to the emerging international evidence on labor market
impacts of AI (del Rio-Chanona et al., 2025). In the United States, Brynjolfsson,
Chandar and Chen (2025); Lichtinger and Hosseini Maasoum (2025); Dominski and
Lee (2025) document employment declines in exposed industries concentrated in
early-career positions. However, Chandar (2025) finds no employment effects using
CPS data, and Humlum and Vestergaard (2025) report null results from Danish
administrative data. These disparities may reflect several factors: differences in
labor market institutions between countries, variation in the timing of LLM adop-
tion across regions, and methodological choices regarding exposure measurement
and outcome variables. Our UK evidence suggests that effects manifest differently
across adjustment margins, with hiring responding more quickly and strongly than
employment stocks.
    Methodologically, our paper builds on recent work that also uses Revelio Labs
data to construct firm-level AI exposure measures. Eisfeldt et al. (2023) show that
higher-exposure US firms experience excess stock returns following the release of
ChatGPT, while Hampole et al. (2025) develop task-level exposure measures varying
across firms and occupations to study US labor demand between 2010 and 2023. We
extend their work in three directions: we shift focus to the UK labor market, examine
realized employment and job posting outcomes (as opposed to Eisfeldt et al. (2023)’s
focus on financial data), and exploit data through mid-2025 to capture labor market
adjustment to generative AI adoption (as opposed to Hampole et al. (2025)’s focus
on earlier waves of AI).
