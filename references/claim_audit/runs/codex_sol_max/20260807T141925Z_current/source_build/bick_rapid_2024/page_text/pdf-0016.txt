time, we also note particular occupations whose adoption rates deviate from what we would
expect based on their exposure score. In particular, office and administrative occupations and
managers are predicted to be similarly exposed to genAI, but actual adoption by managers
is more than double that of office and administrative workers. One potential reason for these
discrepancies could be that existing task-based predictions do not perfectly identify which oc-
cupations would benefit from genAI.

    To investigate this possibility, we leverage additional information about workers’ adoption
decisions from the RPS. Among users who did not adopt genAI, we provided a list of potential
reasons why they did not adopt. We then asked respondents to select the reasons that were
relevant for their decision and to rank them in order of importance. We define the share of
an occupation who does not use genAI because it is not useful for their job as the share of all
workers in that occupation who ranked “GenAI cannot help me with my job” in their top two
reasons for not using genAI . (For the full list of potential reasons, see Appendix A.5.)

    Figure 6c plots genAI adoption against the share who reported that it was not useful. We
find that occupational adoption is highly correlated (-0.88) with the share who reported genAI
was not useful. For example, only 7% of workers in computer / math occupations reported
that genAI was not useful for their job, compared with 30% of personal service workers.
