4.2   Generative AI Use Across Specific Tasks

Given the high correlation between job tasks and adoption, a natural follow up question is to
ask which tasks genAI helps workers complete. We present genAI users with the list of tasks
in Figure 7 and ask them to select any for which they had used genAI in the last week. (They
were also allowed to write in other tasks.) Respondents were then asked to rank these tasks
according to how helpful genAI was in completing them.

    Figure 7 reports the share of respondents who ranked each task in the top two in terms
of importance. The highest ranked tasks at work were writing (45.1%), administrative tasks
(29.9%), and interpreting/translating/summarizing text or data (25.9%). These findings mirror
recent experimental evidence on how genAI reshapes work behavior. Dillon et al. (2025) show
that workers with access to integrated generative AI tools (Microsoft 365 Copilot) spent less
time on email and completed written documents more quickly. At the same time, while genAI
seems most useful for creating, absorbing, and organizing written information, eight of the ten
tasks in our list were ranked by at least 10% of users, indicating a variety of use cases for genAI.
