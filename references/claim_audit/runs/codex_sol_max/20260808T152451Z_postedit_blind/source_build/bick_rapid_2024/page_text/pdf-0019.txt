adding an indicator for employer encouragement dramatically raises the share of variation
explained to 50%. The coefficient on employer encouragement indicates that, holding other
variables constant, a worker is 65 percentage points more likely to use genAI for work if their
employer encourages them to do so. Controlling for employer encouragement also reduces
the magnitude of coefficients on demographic and occupation controls relative to the previous
specification. We caution that the estimated effect of employer encouragement is not necessarily
causal as it may be highly correlated with a worker’s roles and responsibilities (though this is
partially addressed through occupation controls).

    The findings in this section support two conclusions. First, job tasks and responsibilities
appear to be a primary determinant of genAI adoption patterns. Second, workplace barriers
and encouragement also play an important role in a worker’s adoption decision. Hence, when
job tasks are amenable to genAI adoption, firms can play an active role by promoting usage,
offering training, and removing organizational barriers to adoption.
