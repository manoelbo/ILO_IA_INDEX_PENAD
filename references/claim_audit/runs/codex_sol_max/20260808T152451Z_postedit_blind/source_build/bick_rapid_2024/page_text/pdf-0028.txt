6   Conclusion

Predictions about genAI’s economic impact vary widely, driven by uncertainty over the share
of work it can assist and the size of its productivity potential. This paper presents evidence
from the first nationally representative U.S. surveys on generative AI use at work and home.
We find that a substantial share of individuals already use genAI. Thus far, the pace of overall
adoption is rapid compared with past ICT technologies. At work, genAI adoption has followed
a similar pace to PCs and is far ahead of genAI adoption by firms. We document wide variation
in genAI use across demographic groups and occupations, and provide evidence that both task
composition and firm policies appear to play an important role in workers’ adoption choices.
Although the intensity of genAI use varies widely, genAI already assists a noticeable share of
total work hours, suggesting the potential for meaningful productivity gains.

   Our findings help lay the groundwork for understanding the labor market implications of
genAI. They can also inform responses by managers and policymakers along several dimensions,
such as data center and electricity needs, workforce training, and competitive dynamics.
