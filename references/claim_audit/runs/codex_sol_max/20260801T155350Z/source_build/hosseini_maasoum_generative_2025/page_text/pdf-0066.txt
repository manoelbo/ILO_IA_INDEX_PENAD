   Implications. Current automation lowers Γ0 and hence the MPL of labor, reducing n0 .
Anticipated future automation (higher I1 and lower Γ1 ) makes separations more likely at
t = 1. When the firm expects to be in the firing regime tomorrow, the period-0 condition
(24) features the same firing wedge βϕ, which depresses current hiring. Larger firing
costs ϕ strengthen this effect. Consequently, in equilibrium n0C < n0E (where E denotes the
no-firing regime), ∂n0 /∂I0 < 0, and ∂n0 /∂(E0 I1 ) < 0.

   In both formulations, the key force is the intertemporal link created by convex adjust-
ment on the downside: the possibility of paying ϕ tomorrow raises the effective marginal
cost of hiring by βϕ today. Expectations of labor-saving progress therefore reduce current
employment even before automation is realized.
