   When tasks are complementary or Cobb-Douglas (σ ≤ 1), two of the three forces re-
duce junior labor demand: displacement is unambiguously negative and the productivity
effect is negative under any GenAI adoption (α > 0 or δ J > 0). The only countervailing
force—reallocation—depends on the differential augmentation δ J − δS and vanishes en-
tirely when GenAI augments both worker types equally. Junior labor demand therefore
declines whenever the reallocation effect is sufficiently small relative to the combined dis-
placement and productivity effects—for example, when comparative advantage is steep
near x̂ or when the differential augmentation δ J − δS is small.
