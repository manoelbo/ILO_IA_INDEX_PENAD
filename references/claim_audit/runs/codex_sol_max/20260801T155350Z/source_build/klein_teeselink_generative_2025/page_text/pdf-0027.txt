5    Conclusion
This paper provides evidence that large language models are reshaping UK la-
bor markets. Using a difference-in-differences design that exploits variation in
occupational- and firm-level LLM exposure, we conduct three analyses that show
how labor markets change in response to the introduction of generative AI. First,
we examine firm-level employment stocks to understand how workforce size and
composition evolve within organizations. Second, we analyze firm-level hiring pat-
terns to capture forward-looking recruitment decisions that precede employment
adjustments. Third, we investigate occupation-level posting dynamics to identify
economy-wide demand shifts.
    Our firm-level employment analysis documents a 0.3 percent reduction in total
employment per standard deviation increase in LLM exposure, though this effect
only becomes statistically significant approximately 18 months after ChatGPT’s
introduction. This reduction is predominantly concentrated in junior positions,
which decline by 0.4 percent per standard deviation, while senior positions show a
smaller, statistically insignificant 0.2 percent reduction. The resulting compositional
shift reduces the fraction of junior workers by 0.1 percentage point per standard
deviation, mechanically increasing average compensation by £106 as firms retain
proportionally more higher-paid senior staff, though this compensation effect is only
marginally significant.
