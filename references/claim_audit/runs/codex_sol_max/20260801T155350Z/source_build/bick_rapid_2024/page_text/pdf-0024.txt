Notes: Data source are the November 2024 wave of the RPS, ages 18-64 (the August 2024 wave did not ask
about time savings). The sample is employed individuals (RPS, N = 3473). Labor productivity is calculated as
real value added (Bureau of Economic Analysis) divided by employment (Bureau of Labor Statistics) in each
sector. We exclude Agriculture because the productivity cannot be constructed with the standard BLS data,
and Mining, Utilities, Wholesale Trade, and Management industries due to low sample size. Panel (a) plots
industry-wide time savings from genAI against industry-wide hours worked last week using genAI, with both
variables expressed relative to total industry hours worked last week. Panel (b) plots estimated productivity
gains, as defined in Equation (4), against the industry-wide hours using genAI last week relative to total industry
hours worked last week. When calculating the mean share of last week’s work hours spent using genAI, we
weight observations by their last week’s work hours to obtain an aggregate estimate of genAI usage as a share
of hours worked. See text for details on the calculation of estimated productivity gains. Panels (c) and (d)
plot estimated changes in effective labor and productivity against annualized labor productivity growth in the
US over the years 2022 - 2024, detrended by subtracting the annualized growth predicted by the trend over
the period 2015-2019 from the actual growth rate. To minimize the impact of outliers, panels (b), (c), and (d),
which weight time savings using wages, exclude observations with (i) hourly wages below the national minimum
wage of $7.25 or (ii) weekly earnings above $10,000.
